VERSION:        5.1 (addendum to V5.0)

STATUS:         MANDATORY SUPPLEMENT — read with V5.0, not instead of it

HOW TO USE THIS ADDENDUM
------------------------
1. V5.0 remains the base specification. Nothing in V5.0 is withdrawn.
2. Every requirement in this addendum is ADDITIVE and MANDATORY unless marked
   [CONDITIONAL] — conditional items apply only when the stated trigger exists.
3. Where this addendum and V5.0 conflict, this addendum prevails for the specific
   point, and the conflict must be recorded in the Correction Register.
4. New deliverables continue the V5.0 numbering (39 onward).
5. New sign-off questions are added to the V5.0 FINAL SIGN-OFF TEST.
6. All new content carries the V5.0 evidence labels. No new label is introduced.

EVIDENCE LABELS (unchanged from V5.0, restated for clarity)
-----------------------------------------------------------
[LAW]               Operative primary legal instrument.
[OFFICIAL]          Official government/regulator publication.
[VERIFY]            Primary source not retrievable or not independently confirmed.
[PRACTICE]          Bangladeshi operating practice requiring validation.
[BENCHMARK]         International good practice / accreditation benchmark.
[MODEL ASSUMPTION]  Financial, staffing, capacity or planning assumption.
[MODEL CALCULATION] Arithmetic derived from stated assumptions.

New content in this addendum is predominantly [PRACTICE], [BENCHMARK] and
[MODEL ASSUMPTION]. Do not upgrade any of it to [LAW] without a primary
instrument. In particular, the legal regimes listed in Section B are identified
as APPLICABLE REGIMES TO VERIFY — their existence is not in doubt, but their
specific application to a private medical college hospital must be confirmed
against the operative text before any compliance claim is made.


================================================================================
SECTION A — STRUCTURAL ADDITIONS (TIER 1)
================================================================================

These ten items change the shape of the document. Each must appear as a named
section in the master, not as a passing reference.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A1. INSTITUTIONAL STARTING-POSITION DECLARATION  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
V5.0 assumes a greenfield institution. Most real promoters are not greenfield.

REQUIREMENT
The master must open with a mandatory declaration of starting position, and the
entire model must state which pathway applies. The four recognised positions are:

  POSITION 1 — GREENFIELD
    No hospital, no college, land only or land not yet acquired.
    Full Phase -2 to Phase 3 pathway applies.

  POSITION 2 — HOSPITAL-ONLY (OPERATING)
    A licensed, operating hospital with no medical college.
    The statutory hospital-operating precondition may already be running or
    satisfied. The model must state the accumulated operating period, bed
    strength and occupancy evidence, and whether it satisfies the operative
    precondition [VERIFY against the exact section].

  POSITION 3 — COLLEGE-WITHOUT-OWN-HOSPITAL
    An existing college using a third-party or government hospital.
    The model must address: bed-strength adequacy, teaching-space rights,
    access agreements, patient-consent and supervision arrangements, and the
    risk that the arrangement is not recognised for approval or accreditation.

  POSITION 4 — EXISTING COLLEGE SEEKING UPGRADE
    Seat expansion, new programmes, dental unit, nursing institute, or
    postgraduate addition. The model must address: proportional increase in
    land, floor space, beds and infrastructure required when seats or beds
    increase [VERIFY the operative proportionality rule], and grandfathering
    or transitional provisions.

REQUIRED OUTPUT
A one-page "Starting Position Statement" in the master and in the Board paper,
naming the position, the evidence relied on, and the pathway selected.

WHY
Applying the greenfield pathway to an operating hospital produces a wrong
timeline and a wrong financial model. Applying the hospital-only pathway to a
college without its own hospital produces a compliance failure.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A2. FACULTY SUPPLY AND SOURCING STRATEGY  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The model requires a large clinical faculty establishment. Bangladesh does not
have surplus supply. V5.0 sets the ratio but never confronts the constraint.

REQUIREMENT
A dedicated section covering:

  (a) Establishment demand
      Department-by-department sanctioned posts at the modelled seat count,
      split basic science / clinical, and split full-time / permitted part-time.

  (b) Supply reality
      An honest statement that the required full-time clinical faculty is
      unlikely to be available in the open market at prevailing public-sector
      pay, and that most private colleges operate on borrowed or visiting
      faculty — which is precisely what the part-time ceiling restricts.

  (c) Sourcing routes, each with its own permission and evidence requirements:
      1. Open-market recruitment of experienced consultants.
      2. Government deputation / lien / no-objection route — state that this
         requires the releasing authority's permission and record the evidence
         required [VERIFY current rules].
      3. Returning Bangladeshi physicians and diaspora recruitment.
      4. Own pipeline: residents and registrars developed into faculty over
         time, with the years-to-competence assumption stated.
      5. Academic partnership or shared-appointment arrangements with a public
         college or university [VERIFY permissibility].
      6. Part-time and visiting appointments strictly within the statutory
         ceiling.

  (d) Consequence of shortfall
      An explicit, pre-agreed rule for what happens if the establishment cannot
      be filled: reduce sanctioned seats, delay intake, defer the programme, or
      seek a time-bound remediation plan. The rule must name who decides and
      what the regulator is told.

  (e) Faculty retention
      Pay positioning, protected teaching time, research support, clinical
      facility access, and career progression — with the retention risk stated.

REQUIRED OUTPUT
A Faculty Supply Plan with a year-by-year fill trajectory, not a single
end-state number.

WHY
A model that specifies a ratio it cannot staff is not implementable. The
shortfall rule is the single most useful control in this section, because it
converts an aspiration into a decision.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A3. FEE REGULATION AS A STRUCTURAL CONSTRAINT  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
V5.0 treats the government fee instrument as an input source. It is a ceiling.

REQUIREMENT
State plainly, in the financial section and in the Board paper:

  (a) Admission and tuition fees for private medical and dental colleges are
      fixed by government instrument (MEFWD Medical Education-1: 649 is the
      identified instrument; verify the operative version and table) [VERIFY
      exact current table].

  (b) College revenue is therefore a REGULATED number, not a management
      variable. The institution cannot price its way out of a cost problem.

  (c) Consequence: the financial viability of the project rests predominantly
      on hospital operations, not on college tuition. The hospital is the
      commercial engine; the college is a regulated-margin academic obligation.

  (d) Any financial model that assumes fee growth above the gazetted schedule
      is invalid and must be rejected at the arithmetic gate.

  (e) Ancillary charges, hostel, food and other permitted services must be
      modelled only where legally permitted, and separately.

REQUIRED OUTPUT
A short "Fee Constraint Statement" in the financial section, and a matching
paragraph in the Board paper's financial envelope.

WHY
This single fact reorders the project's risk profile. A Board that believes it
can raise fees to cover a shortfall will approve a project it should decline.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A4. PRE-COLLEGE CASH VALLEY — NAMED AND SIZED  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The statutory pathway requires the hospital to operate for a defined period at
a defined occupancy before the college may apply. That period generates cost
with no college revenue.

REQUIREMENT
The financial model must isolate and report, as a named output:

  "PHASE -1 CUMULATIVE CASH REQUIREMENT (NO COLLEGE REVENUE)"

covering:
  - hospital operating cost during the qualifying period;
  - occupancy ramp assumptions and the month occupancy is expected to cross
    the required threshold;
  - capital servicing during the period;
  - working capital consumed;
  - cumulative cash peak (the deepest point);
  - month the peak occurs;
  - committed funding available at that month;
  - headroom, expressed in months of operating cost.

The Board paper must show the peak figure and the headroom in one line.

WHY
This is the point at which projects fail. It is also the number a lender or
investor will ask for first. V5.0 requires hospital-first sequencing but never
requires the cash valley to be quantified.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A5. MINIMUM VIABLE CONFIGURATION  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
V5.0 models 100 seats / 500 beds. The statutory floor is lower. A promoter may
only be able to fund the floor.

REQUIREMENT
Model TWO configurations side by side:

  CONFIGURATION A — STATUTORY MINIMUM
    Minimum permitted seats, minimum permitted land, minimum permitted floor
    space, minimum permitted bed strength and occupancy, minimum staffing to
    satisfy the ratio and the part-time ceiling [VERIFY each minimum against
    the operative instrument].

  CONFIGURATION B — TARGET STATE
    The 100-seat / 500-bed model already specified in V5.0.

For each configuration report: land, core capex, opex, staffing headcount,
revenue at base/downside/severe, break-even, and the pre-college cash valley.

Report the DELTA between A and B explicitly.

REQUIRED OUTPUT
A two-column comparison table in the financial section and in the Board paper.

WHY
A document that only describes an unaffordable target state is unusable by the
majority of its intended readers. The delta also shows the Board what each
increment of scale actually costs.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A6. FAILURE AND EXIT SCENARIOS  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The model describes only success. A Board paper that cannot describe failure is
not a Board paper.

REQUIREMENT
A dedicated section covering at least these six scenarios, each with trigger,
consequence, decision rights, and protective actions:

  1. INITIAL POLICY APPROVAL REFUSED
     Grounds, appeal or re-application route, sunk-cost position, land
     disposition, staff liabilities.

  2. ACADEMIC APPROVAL REFUSED OR DELAYED
     Effect on hospital-only operation, funding covenants, staff retention.

  3. ACCREDITATION FAILED OR CERTIFICATE SUSPENDED
     Effect on student recruitment, foreign-student channel, marketing claims,
     remediation plan, appeal route.

  4. PROMOTER INSOLVENCY OR FUNDING WITHDRAWAL MID-CONSTRUCTION
     Asset protection, secured-creditor position, completion options,
     regulatory notification duty.

  5. HOSPITAL UNABLE TO REACH REQUIRED OCCUPANCY
     Consequence for college eligibility, options (service-line change,
     partnership, deferral), and the decision deadline.

  6. ADVERSE REGULATOR ACTION
     Licence suspension, admission freeze, seat reduction, penalty. Immediate
     obligations, patient-transfer duty, student-protection duty, public
     communication.

ADDITIONALLY, for every scenario state:
  - obligations to students already admitted (teaching continuity, fee refund,
    transfer assistance, examination access);
  - obligations to staff (notice, gratuity, provident fund, certificate release);
  - obligations to patients (continuity of care, record transfer);
  - who decides, and whether the decision is Board-reserved.

WHY
Student-protection obligations in particular are legally and reputationally
binding and are almost always omitted from promoter-side models.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A7. AFFILIATION STRATEGY  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
V5.0 treats "affiliating public university" as a given. It is a strategic choice
with multi-decade consequences.

REQUIREMENT
A section covering:

  (a) Candidate universities and their current capacity to affiliate new
      private colleges [VERIFY current position with each university].

  (b) Selection criteria: syllabus version and currency, examination calendar
      and result timelines, inspection intensity and frequency, fee structure,
      geographic proximity, willingness to affiliate, and reputation with
      regulators and employers.

  (c) Negotiation and agreement: what the affiliation instrument must contain
      (curriculum version, examination responsibilities, inspection rights,
      Governing Body representation, dispute resolution, termination).

  (d) Operational consequences: examination scheduling dependency, result
      delay risk, syllabus change risk, and the cost of switching.

  (e) Contingency: what happens if the chosen university declines, withdraws,
      or changes its syllabus mid-cohort.

WHY
Examination timing and result publication are controlled by the university, not
the college. A college that cannot predict its own result dates cannot plan
internship intake, graduation, or BMDC registration.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A8. FOREIGN STUDENTS  [CONDITIONAL — applies if foreign admission is intended]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Absent from V5.0 entirely. For many private colleges this is the difference
between viability and closure.

REQUIREMENT
A section covering:

  (a) Legal basis and permitted proportion of foreign students relative to
      sanctioned seats [VERIFY the operative proportionality rule].

  (b) Admission channel: separate merit/eligibility process, document
      equivalence, and the MEFWD certificate-verification desk process.

  (c) Fee treatment: whether foreign-student fees are separately regulated or
      institutionally set [VERIFY].

  (d) Regulatory reporting: student lists to DGME, registration with BMDC,
      visa and stay compliance, and the consequences of over-admission.

  (e) Academic support: language of instruction, Bangla-language clinical
      exposure, bridging support, and examination-language rules.

  (f) Reputational and diplomatic risk: home-country recognition of the
      Bangladeshi degree, embassy engagement, and complaint handling across
      jurisdictions.

  (g) Revenue modelling: separate line, separate collection risk, separate
      refund policy.

WHY
Foreign-student revenue is often the margin that makes a private college work,
and foreign-student failure is the fastest route to a diplomatic incident and a
recruitment collapse.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A9. REGULATOR ENGAGEMENT AND INSPECTION READINESS  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
V5.0 lists the Inspection Committee's composition but never says what it
examines or how to prepare.

REQUIREMENT
A section covering:

  (a) Pre-application engagement sequence with DGME: what to submit, in what
      order, what informal clarification is appropriate, and what must never be
      represented before approval.

  (b) Inspection readiness pack — what the committee will actually look for:
      - land title and mutation documents, unencumbered and undivided;
      - approved building plans and occupancy certification;
      - floor-space measurement evidence, academic and hospital separately;
      - bed strength, occupancy records and case-mix evidence;
      - department-wise faculty presence, credentials and registration;
      - laboratory, library, skill-lab and lecture-space demonstration;
      - hospital services in operation, not merely constructed;
      - free-bed designation and utilisation evidence;
      - reserved-fund bank evidence;
      - field-site agreements (health complex and community clinic);
      - student records, admission compliance and fee compliance.

  (c) Physical demonstration requirements: what must be functioning on the day,
      not merely present.

  (d) Common failure points observed in practice [PRACTICE]: faculty shown on
      paper but absent; hospital built but not operating; floor space counted
      across rented or shared areas; occupancy asserted without records;
      free beds designated but not actually free.

  (e) Post-inspection obligations: responding to findings, corrective action
      evidence, and the re-inspection route.

WHY
The gap between "we have the building" and "we pass inspection" is where most
applications fail. Naming the failure points in advance is the cheapest
mitigation available.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A10. DELIVERABLE PRODUCTION PLAN  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
V5.0 lists 38 deliverables with no owner, order, acceptance criteria or minimum
viable subset. This is the same defect previously identified in the master's
own implementation section.

REQUIREMENT
For every deliverable, specify:
  - owner (named role, not a department);
  - author and reviewer;
  - prerequisite deliverables;
  - acceptance criteria (what "done" means, testably);
  - target date relative to a phase gate;
  - approval authority.

AND define three mandatory subsets:

  SUBSET 1 — BEFORE FIRST BOARD MEETING (minimum 5)
    Governance Constitution; Reserved Matters Schedule; Delegation of Authority;
    Regulatory Register; Conflict-of-Interest Register.

  SUBSET 2 — BEFORE HOSPITAL OPENING (minimum 20)
    All of Subset 1 plus: licensing tracker; clinical governance pack; nursing
    credentialing pack; patient rights and SLA pack; free-bed control pack;
    revenue-integrity pack; procurement segregation matrix; HR credentialing
    plan; digital/HIS architecture; data-governance pack; cybersecurity
    baseline; BCP/DR and incident command; enterprise risk register; Board
    dashboard; CAPEX/OPEX assumptions register; funding stack and liquidity
    model.

  SUBSET 3 — BEFORE ACCREDITATION APPLICATION (all 38 plus this addendum's
    additions)
    Complete set, with the accreditation evidence matrix fully populated.

WHY
Thirty-eight unowned documents is a wish list. Five owned documents with
acceptance criteria is a plan.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A11. BOARD EFFECTIVENESS  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
V5.0 specifies Board control instruments but not Board quality.

REQUIREMENT
A section covering:
  - Board skills matrix and the gaps it reveals;
  - independent / non-executive members and their proportion;
  - tenure limits and rotation;
  - annual Board self-evaluation, with method and reporting;
  - Audit & Risk Committee composition, independence and rotation;
  - director induction and continuing education;
  - declaration and management of interests, annually and event-based;
  - meeting effectiveness: agenda discipline, paper deadlines, minutes quality,
    action tracking;
  - succession and emergency leadership provisions (already required by V5.0 —
    here specify the trigger and the interim authority).

WHY
Every control in the document depends on a Board capable of exercising it. A
weak Board makes the Reserved Matters Schedule decorative.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A12. DOCUMENT CHANGE CONTROL  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REQUIREMENT
  - named owner of the master document and of each deliverable;
  - version numbering and change-log discipline;
  - review cycle (at least annual, and on any material regulatory change);
  - approval authority for amendments (Board-reserved for constitutional
    sections);
  - the procedure by which a newly gazetted instrument is absorbed mid-project,
    including impact assessment, re-approval and communication;
  - archival of superseded versions with retrieval dates;
  - a rule that no superseded version may be circulated as current.

WHY
A 4,000-line document with no change control becomes unreliable within one
regulatory cycle. Bangladesh's medical-education rules are actively moving —
three draft rule versions are already listed on the ministry index.


================================================================================
SECTION B — BANGLADESH LEGAL REGIMES TO ADD
================================================================================

These regimes are real and current. Their existence is not in doubt; their
specific application to a private medical college hospital must be confirmed
against the operative text before any compliance claim is made. Treat each as
[VERIFY] until confirmed, and design the institution to comply regardless.

For each regime the master must state: applicability trigger, the obligation,
the responsible owner, the evidence retained, and the inspection authority.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B1. SAFE BLOOD TRANSFUSION ACT, 2002
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRIGGER: any blood bank or transfusion service.
REQUIRE: licensing basis, donor selection and screening, cold chain, storage,
cross-match, traceability, transfusion-reaction reporting, haemovigilance,
discard control, and the responsible transfusion officer.
NOTE: listed on the MEFWD law index; confirm the operative text and current
licensing authority.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B2. BREAST-MILK SUBSTITUTES (REGULATION OF MARKETING) ORDINANCE, 1984
    AND THE 1993 RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRIGGER: any maternity, obstetric or neonatal service; any paediatric teaching.
REQUIRE: prohibition on promotion of breast-milk substitutes within the
institution; no free samples, no branded materials, no company representatives
in clinical areas; staff training; Baby-Friendly-aligned practice; complaint
route; audit.
NOTE: this is a marketing-prohibition regime with real enforcement history.
It applies to a teaching hospital with an obstetrics department and to the
college's community-medicine field activities.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B3. RIGHTS AND PROTECTION OF PERSONS WITH DISABILITIES ACT, 2013
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRIGGER: all institutions.
REQUIRE: physical accessibility of college and hospital (entrances, ramps,
lifts, toilets, signage, seating, parking); accessible information; admission
and employment non-discrimination; reasonable accommodation; complaint route.
NOTE: accessibility is also an accreditation and patient-experience issue, so
the requirement is doubly grounded.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B4. SEXUAL HARASSMENT — 2009 HIGH COURT GUIDELINES (BLAST v BANGLADESH)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRIGGER: every workplace and every educational institution.
REQUIRE: a constituted complaints committee with the prescribed composition
(including an external member); a written procedure; confidentiality;
protection against retaliation; annual reporting; awareness training for staff
and students.
NOTE: this is a judicial mandate, not an optional policy. It is frequently
absent in private institutions and is a live enforcement risk.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B5. RAGGING PROHIBITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRIGGER: every educational institution with students.
REQUIRE: a written anti-ragging policy; a reporting route that does not depend
on the accused's department; a disciplinary procedure; a police-reporting
protocol for serious cases; orientation for new students; monitoring of
hostels and clinical placements.
NOTE: V5.0 requires an anti-ragging committee. This addendum requires the
protocol behind it, including the external-reporting route.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B6. DEATH CERTIFICATION, MEDICO-LEGAL AUTOPSY AND ORGAN DONATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRIGGER: any hospital with a mortuary; any hospital receiving medico-legal
cases.
REQUIRE: death certification procedure and authorised certifiers; medico-legal
case identification and police interface; autopsy referral; mortuary register
and chain of custody; body release procedure; dignity standards; organ and
tissue donation framework under the applicable 1999 law [VERIFY operative
text]; staff training; family communication.
NOTE: V5.0 mentions the mortuary once, in a support-services list. The
medico-legal interface is a significant source of institutional risk.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B7. SMOKING AND TOBACCO CONTROL ACT, 2005
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRIGGER: all public places, including hospitals and educational institutions.
REQUIRE: designated no-smoking status across the campus; signage; enforcement;
staff and visitor communication; cessation support signposting.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B8. MEDICAL DEVICE REGULATION (DGDA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRIGGER: any institution importing, storing or using medical devices.
REQUIRE: import and registration compliance; approved-device list; calibration
and maintenance records; adverse-event and malfunction reporting; recall
handling; traceability for implants; disposal of expired devices.
NOTE: implant traceability is also a clinical-governance and patient-safety
requirement, so the control serves two purposes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B9. ADDITIONAL REGIMES TO VERIFY AND ADDRESS IF APPLICABLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Mental Health Act, 2018 — involuntary admission, consent, restraint,
    rights of persons with mental illness [VERIFY applicability to any
    psychiatry inpatient service].
  - Organ Transplant Act, 1999 — living and deceased donation, authorisation,
    committee requirements.
  - Domestic Violence (Prevention and Protection) Act, 2010 — screening and
    referral duty for a hospital receiving such cases.
  - Prevention of Oppression against Women and Children Act, 2000 — forensic
    examination, evidence preservation, reporting duty.
  - Labour Act, 2006 and rules — working hours, leave, maternity benefit,
    provident fund, gratuity, occupational safety, and the special provisions
    for shops and establishments.
  - Bangladesh Labour Rules — registers, service rules, welfare provisions.
  - Income Tax Act and VAT regime — withholding, VAT on services, exemption
    treatment for educational and medical services [VERIFY current treatment].
  - Bangladesh Accreditation Board — ISO 15189 laboratory accreditation
    [BENCHMARK unless voluntarily adopted].
  - Fire Prevention and Extinguishment Act and rules — NOC, drills, equipment,
    evacuation.
  - Bangladesh Environment Conservation Act, 1995 and Medical Waste Management
    Rules, 2008 — clearance, segregation, treatment, disposal, records.
  - Nuclear Safety and Radiation Control Act, 1993 — licensing, radiation
    safety officer, dosimetry, shielding.
  - Drugs Act, 1940 and Drugs Rules, 1945 — pharmacy licensing, narcotics,
    record-keeping.
  - Medical Practice and Private Clinics and Laboratories (Regulation)
    Ordinance, 1982 — hospital and clinic licensing basis [VERIFY current
    operative status and any successor instrument].

SECTION C — METHOD ADDITIONS (TIER 3)
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C1. JUST-CULTURE AND INCIDENT TAXONOMY  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
V5.0 requires incident reporting. It does not define what must be reported.

REQUIRE:
  - a severity scale (near miss / no harm / minor / moderate / major / sentinel);
  - a defined sentinel-event list for a teaching hospital;
  - reporting thresholds and timeframes by severity;
  - the no-blame boundary: what is treated as system failure, what is treated as
    reckless or intentional conduct, and who decides;
  - duty of candour: what is disclosed to the patient or family, by whom, when,
    and how it is recorded;
  - the review body for each severity level;
  - the escalation route to the Board and the notification duty to a regulator
    where applicable;
  - protection for reporters;
  - the learning loop: how a finding changes a protocol, a roster or a resource.

WHY
Without a taxonomy, "incident reporting" produces either nothing or noise, and
the institution cannot demonstrate a safety culture to an accreditation surveyor.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C2. BANGLA LANGUAGE STANDARD  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REQUIRE: a stated standard that all patient-facing material is available in
Bangla — consent forms, discharge instructions, medication directions, signage,
wayfinding, price information, complaint routes, visiting rules, and patient
rights. English may be provided additionally. Clinical documentation may follow
the institution's record standard, but patient communication must be in a
language the patient understands, with interpreter support where the patient's
first language is neither Bangla nor English.

WHY
This is a patient-rights requirement, a consent-validity issue, and a
patient-experience metric. It is also routinely omitted.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C3. CONSENT AND CAPACITY — OPERATING RULES  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bangladesh has no comprehensive capacity statute. The institution therefore
needs an operating rule, not a legal citation.

REQUIRE, with a written procedure for each:
  - adults with capacity;
  - minors, and the age at which consent transfers, with the accompanying
    assent requirement;
  - patients lacking capacity: who may consent, in what order of priority, and
    how the decision is recorded;
  - emergency treatment without consent, and its limits;
  - unconscious patients and unidentified patients;
  - medico-legal cases and the police interface;
  - refusal of treatment, including refusal by a patient with capacity whose
    decision the clinician considers unwise;
  - consent for student involvement in care, and the patient's right to decline
    without any effect on their treatment;
  - consent for photography, recording and teaching material;
  - consent for research, separately from consent for treatment;
  - documentation standard and retention.

WHY
Student involvement in care is the consent issue most specific to a teaching
hospital, and the one most often left undocumented.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C4. STUDENT MENTAL HEALTH AND SUICIDE-PREVENTION PROTOCOL  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REQUIRE:
  - a named responsible officer for student wellbeing;
  - confidential access to counselling, with a stated response time;
  - a risk-assessment and escalation procedure for a student in crisis;
  - a hostel and residential monitoring arrangement;
  - a protocol for a student death or serious self-harm event, covering family
    notification, peer support, academic continuity and record handling;
  - anti-stigma training for faculty and class teachers;
  - a clear boundary between academic mentoring and clinical mental-health care,
    with referral routes;
  - confidentiality rules and their limits.

WHY
This is a documented and recurring problem in Bangladeshi medical colleges.
Counselling is mentioned in V5.0; a protocol is not.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C5. PATIENT AND FAMILY COMMUNICATION STANDARDS  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REQUIRE, beyond the SLA list in V5.0:
  - who delivers bad news, and the minimum standard for doing so;
  - structured family briefing for ICU and critical care, with frequency;
  - how a change in condition is communicated and recorded;
  - how a complaint is explained back to the complainant;
  - how a billing dispute is explained, by whom, and in what language;
  - how a death is communicated, and the immediate practical support offered;
  - interpreter availability and response time;
  - the rule that no patient learns of a serious finding from a bill or a
    corridor conversation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C6. SUPPLY-CHAIN AND ESSENTIAL-MEDICINE CONTINUITY  [MANDATORY]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REQUIRE:
  - a defined essential-medicines and consumables list;
  - minimum stock levels and reorder points for each;
  - approved alternative suppliers for each critical item;
  - a stock-out escalation procedure with clinical notification;
  - oxygen supply resilience: primary, backup, and the duration of each;
  - blood-supply contingency;
  - a substitution protocol that requires clinical approval;
  - a rule that a stock-out is reported as an incident, not absorbed silently.

WHY
Oxygen and essential-medicine failure is the most common cause of avoidable
death in a hospital emergency, and the least discussed in governance documents.

SECTION D — ONE CHANGE TO V5.0
================================================================================

D1. BOARD PAPER LENGTH — SET A HARD TARGET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
V5.0 says the Board paper should be "approximately 15–25 pages". The delivered
Board paper was 39 lines — roughly 1% of the master.

REQUIREMENT
  - Hard target: 12–18 pages.
  - Readable in 40 minutes by a director who has not read the master.
  - Must contain, at minimum: purpose; the decisions sought with the exact
    resolution wording; the five authorities; the three lines; reserved matters
    summary; delegation summary; executive structure; regulatory gates;
    financial envelope including land; the corrected three-scenario revenue
    model with the arithmetic shown; the pre-college cash valley; the funding
    gates; the hospital-first sequence; the 20 dashboard indicators; the top
    ten risks; the failure scenarios in summary; and the sign-off test.
  - Must reference the master for detail rather than duplicate appendices.
  - Must be accompanied by a one-page decision sheet that a director can sign.

WHY
A Board paper too short to carry the decisions fails as surely as one too long
to be read. The 39-line version could not support a capital decision.
